The Overnight

Eleven hours, mostly spent waiting.

Nothing here is fast. Einkorn ferments slowly and stays slack, so the schedule is built around the dough rather than the clock. Times are illustrative.

  1. 20:00

    Mix

    Flour, water, salt and a spoonful of starter that was itself fed this morning. No commercial yeast, no conditioners, nothing to speed it along.

  2. 22:30

    Fold

    A few gentle folds, half an hour apart, to build what little structure einkorn will give you. Push it harder and the dough tears instead of tightening.

  3. 02:00

    Shape

    Shaped by hand and set to rest cold. This is the hour that makes the flavor — the long, quiet part nobody sees.

  4. 04:30

    Bake

    Scored and baked dark in a home oven, one batch at a time. The crust goes further than most people expect, and that is on purpose.

  5. 07:00

    On the counter

    Cooled just enough to slice. Pickup starts here, and on a good week it is gone by noon.

The Grain

The wheat we ate for ten thousand years.

Einkorn is the oldest cultivated wheat on earth, and the only one never crossed, bred, or reworked into something else.

Modern wheat replaced it for reasons that had nothing to do with flavor. Einkorn yields less per acre, holds a stubborn hull, and its dough is slack and unfamiliar under a machine. The industry picked the grain that suited the equipment.

We picked the other one, and then built the whole night around it.

  • 10,000 years of people eating einkorn before anyone thought to improve it
  • 100% einkorn in every loaf, with no modern flour blended in to make it easier
  • 3 ingredients — flour, water, salt. The fourth is time, and time is free

Einkorn is not gluten-free.

Einkorn contains gluten — an older, structurally different form of it, but gluten all the same. If you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy, our bread is not safe for you. We would rather lose a sale than mislead you. We are also bakers, not doctors: nothing here is medical advice.

Questions

Fair questions, straight answers.

Is einkorn gluten-free?

No. Einkorn contains gluten. Its gluten is structurally different from modern wheat’s and some people report tolerating it better, but if you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy einkorn is not safe for you — full stop.

Why does it cost more than supermarket bread?

Einkorn yields a fraction of what modern wheat does per acre, its tight hull needs extra processing, and very few farms grow it. Add slow natural fermentation and hand shaping and each loaf simply takes more of everything — land, time, and labor.

What does einkorn bread taste like?

Sweeter and nuttier than regular wheat, with a golden crumb that comes from the grain’s own carotenoids. The texture is a touch denser and more tender than fluffy commercial bread — closer to what bread was for most of human history.

Do you ship?

Not yet. As a Connecticut cottage-food bakery we sell locally, with pickup on bake days. If demand grows, a bigger kitchen — and shipping — may follow.
