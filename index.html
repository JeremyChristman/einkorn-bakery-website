Our Story

A pastry chef, a home oven, and one stubborn grain.

Will has spent his career as a professional chef and pastry chef. Somewhere between restaurant kitchens and thousands of loaves, he found einkorn — the original wheat, unchanged for ten thousand years — and couldn't understand why almost nobody was baking with it.

The answer, it turned out, wasn't flavor or nutrition. Einkorn lost out because it's harder to grow, harder to mill, and harder to bake with at industrial speed. The food industry chose the grain that fit the machines. We've been eating the consequences ever since.

Ancient Grains is Will's answer: a small home bakery doing the slow thing on purpose. Real sourdough. One ancient grain. And a standing invitation to learn why it matters.

Why Einkorn

The wheat we ate for 10,000 years — until we didn't.

Einkorn is the oldest cultivated wheat on Earth, and the only one that was never hybridized. Here's what changed, and why we think it's worth going back.

The original grain

Einkorn (Triticum monococcum) was domesticated in the Fertile Crescent over 10,000 years ago. It's genetically the simplest wheat there is — two sets of chromosomes, where modern bread wheat carries six.

What changed

Twentieth-century wheat was bred for yield, uniformity, and machine baking — not nutrition or flavor. Then roller mills stripped away the bran and germ, leaving fast-digesting refined starch. Bread got cheaper, softer, and emptier.

What einkorn kept

Because it was never bred for industry, einkorn still bakes, tastes, and nourishes the way wheat used to — a golden crumb, a sweet nutty flavor, and a nutritional profile modern flour can't match.

More protein

Published compositional analyses consistently find einkorn higher in protein than modern bread wheat — commonly around 25–30% more.

Rich in carotenoids

Einkorn is one of the best wheat sources of lutein, the carotenoid that gives its flour a golden color — several times the level found in modern wheat.

More minerals

Studies report higher levels of several essential minerals — including zinc, iron, and phosphorus — compared with common wheat.

A simpler gluten

Einkorn's gluten is structurally different: it lacks the D-genome proteins found in modern wheat. Some people who find conventional bread hard on their stomach tell us einkorn sits easier — the science here is still young, so we call that what it is: promising, and anecdotal.

Slow sourdough, on top

Every loaf is naturally leavened for 18+ hours. Slow, wild fermentation is how bakers prepared grain for millennia — and along the way it partially breaks down starches and phytic acid.

Nothing else

Flour, water, salt, and time. No dough conditioners, no preservatives, no enrichment needed — the grain brings its own.

We're bakers, not doctors. Nothing on this page is medical advice, and no bread — however ancient the wheat — treats or prevents any disease. If you have a health condition, talk to your physician.

The honest part: einkorn is not gluten-free.

Einkorn contains gluten — a different, older form of it, but gluten all the same. If you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy, our bread is not safe for you. We'd rather lose a sale than mislead you; that's the whole point of this bakery.

FAQ

Fair questions, straight answers.

Is einkorn gluten-free?

No. Einkorn contains gluten. Its gluten is structurally different from modern wheat's and some people report tolerating it better, but if you have celiac disease or a wheat allergy, einkorn is not safe for you — full stop.

Why does einkorn bread cost more than supermarket bread?

Einkorn yields a fraction of what modern wheat does per acre, the grain has a tight hull that needs extra processing, and very few farms grow it. Add slow natural fermentation and hand-shaping, and each loaf simply takes more of everything — land, time, and labor. You're tasting where the money went.

What does einkorn bread taste like?

Sweeter and nuttier than regular wheat, with a golden-colored crumb from its natural carotenoids. The texture is a touch denser and more tender than fluffy commercial bread — closer to what bread tasted like for most of human history.

Is everything really 100% einkorn?

Yes — including the sourdough starter itself. No blending with modern wheat flour to make the dough easier to handle. It's harder to bake this way, which is exactly why almost nobody does it.

How should I store the bread?

Cut-side down on the counter under a towel for 2–3 days, or slice and freeze — einkorn sourdough freezes and re-toasts beautifully. Please don't refrigerate it; the fridge stales bread faster.

Do you ship?

Not yet. As a Connecticut cottage-food bakery we sell locally, with pickup on bake days. If demand grows, a bigger kitchen — and shipping — may follow.
